# **Vehicle Direction/Speed (VDS) – PGN 65256 (0x18FEE8FE) in the J1939 Protocol**
## **Introduction**
The **Vehicle Direction/Speed (VDS) – Parameter Group Number (PGN) 65256 (0x18FEE8FE)** is a message in the **SAE J1939 protocol** used in commercial vehicle and off-highway communication. It provides important information about the **vehicle's movement**, including **altitude, inclination, speed, and compass direction**. This data is particularly relevant for **vehicle navigation systems, telematics, and driver assistance systems**.

## **Structure of the VDS Message**

The VDS message has a **Data Length (DLC) of 8 bytes** and is typically transmitted at **1-second intervals (CycleTime = 1000 ms)**. It contains four main parameters:

### **1. SPN-580 - Altitude (height above sea level)**
- **Position:** 48 bits (6 bytes), 16-bit resolution
- **Unit:** Meters (m)
- **Factor:** 0.125
- **Offset:** -2500
- **Range:** -2500 m to +5531.875 m
- **SPN (Suspect Parameter Number):** 580

The altitude refers to **sea level under standard atmospheric conditions** (ISO 2533). The -2500 m offset allows for the representation of low-lying areas (e.g., below sea level).

### **2. SPN-583 - Pitch (Angle of Inclination)**
- **Position:** 32 bits (4 bytes), 16-bit resolution
- **Unit:** Degrees (°)
- **Factor:** 0.0078125 (1/128)
- **Offset:** -200
- **Range:** -200° to +301.9921875°
- **SPN:** 583

The **pitch angle** describes the **rotation around the transverse axis (Y-axis)** of the vehicle. A positive value indicates **ascent (going uphill)**, a negative value **decline (going downhill)**. The definition follows a **Z-down coordinate system**, which is common in aviation and navigation.

### **3. SPN-517 - Navigation-Based Vehicle Speed**
- **Position:** 16 bits (2 bytes), 16-bit resolution
- **Unit:** km/h
- **Factor:** 0.00390625 (1/256)
- **Range:** 0 to 250.99609375 km/h
- **SPN:** 517

This speed is determined **not via wheel sensors**, but via **navigation systems such as GPS**. It is particularly useful when the wheel speed is inaccurate (e.g., due to wheel slippage or tire pressure loss).

### **4. SPN-165 - Compass Bearing (Compass Direction)**
- **Position:** 0 bits (first byte), 16-bit resolution
- **Unit:** Degrees (°)
- **Factor:** 0.0078125 (1/128)
- **Range:** 0° to 501.9921875° (modulo 360° for true compass values)
- **SPN:** 165

The **compass direction** indicates the **current direction of travel** of the vehicle (0° = north, 90° = east, etc.). The value can exceed 360° but is typically normalized to 0°–360°.

## **Areas of Application**

The VDS message is used in various systems, including:

- **Telematics Systems** (e.g., Fleet Management)
- **Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS)**
- **Off-Road and Construction Equipment Control**
- **Autonomous Vehicles & Precision Farming**

Since speed and direction are **GPS-based**, this message is particularly suitable for applications where **wheel encoders would be inaccurate** (e.g., on slippery surfaces).

## **Summary**
The **VDS-PGN 65256 (0x18FEE8FE)** is an important J1939 message that provides the **altitude, pitch, speed, and direction** of a vehicle. Due to its GPS-based speed measurement and precise angle information, it is particularly valuable for **navigation and automation systems**. The defined SPNs enable easy integration into existing J1939 networks, making them a central element of modern vehicle communication.
